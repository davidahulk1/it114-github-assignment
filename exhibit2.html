Exhibit II

Technology and Warfare

As warfare evolved during the twentieth century, naval intelligence became increasingly dependent on technology. Radar, sonar, cryptography, and electronic communication systems transformed the way naval forces detected threats, coordinated missions, and protected national security.

These innovations allowed military organizations to gather information more quickly, track enemy movement across large distances, and improve strategic decision-making during major conflicts and global operations.

Technological Breakthroughs

Radar Systems

Radar systems changed naval warfare by allowing ships to detect enemy aircraft, vessels, and movement across long distances. This technology improved visibility during combat and increased reaction time during military operations.

Radar became especially important during wartime because commanders could identify threats even during poor weather conditions or nighttime operations.

Cryptography

Cryptography protected classified military communication through encryption and coded messaging systems. Secure communication prevented enemy interception and protected sensitive intelligence information.

During wartime, cryptographic systems became critical because military success often depended on keeping operational plans and strategic communication secret.

Sonar Technology

Sonar systems helped naval forces detect submarines and underwater movement. This technology improved maritime defense and underwater navigation capabilities.

Sonar became one of the most important tools for submarine warfare because it allowed ships to identify hidden threats beneath the ocean surface.

Museum Reflection

This exhibit demonstrates how rapidly advancing technology reshaped naval intelligence. Information could now move faster, travel farther, and be analyzed more effectively than ever before.

Radar, sonar, and cryptography expanded the Navy’s ability to monitor threats, coordinate missions, and protect national interests during both wartime and peacetime operations.
