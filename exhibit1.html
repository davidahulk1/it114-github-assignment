Exhibit I

Early Naval Intelligence

Before advanced radar, satellites, and cybersecurity, naval intelligence depended on navigation, communication, observation, and strategy. Sailors used maps, coded signals, signal flags, stars, and ship movement tracking to understand the sea and gain tactical advantage.

Key Intelligence Methods

Navigation Maps

Maps helped commanders understand routes, coastlines, risks, and strategic movement at sea.

Signal Flags

Ships used visual flag systems to send commands and warnings before modern radio systems.

Observation

Lookouts and commanders studied enemy ships, weather, and sea routes to support decisions.

Museum Reflection

This exhibit shows that intelligence began with human observation and communication. Even without digital systems, early naval forces used information as a weapon of strategy.
